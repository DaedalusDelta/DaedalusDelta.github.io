Geometric fabrics: a mathematical reference

This reference reconstructs the core geometry, mechanics, energization, composition, constraint, and stability arguments needed to design geometric fabrics. It fixes one notation across the original bent-Finsler formulation and the later conservative-system formulation. Proofs are supplied rather than delegated to citations. The companion tutorial must explain these same results; prerequisites and intermediate algebra may be expanded, but hypotheses must not be weakened.

Scope. The subject is the mathematical motion-generation framework, including its design and numerical limits. It is not a reproduction of the learning experiments in the 2024 application. Exact continuous-time identities, conditional convergence results, and engineering approximations are distinguished. The intended reader should be able to reconstruct the results, identify their assumptions, and implement a conforming example.

Logical dependency. The two design goals are to preserve a geometry and to select timing along it. R1 establishes tangential retiming of HD2 paths without any energy assumption. R2 supplies an energy-rate identity; R3 uses that identity to select a conservative representative of the already chosen geometry. R3c makes its clock explicit. The mechanics in R0a motivates the energy tools, but conservation is not a premise of R1. The tutorial follows this dependency order, introducing geometry before the supporting mechanics interlude.

Primary sources: G21: Generalized Nonlinear and Finsler Geometry for Robotics; F22: Geometric Fabrics: Generalizing Classical Mechanics to Capture the Physics of Behavior; F23: Fabrics: A Foundationally Stable Medium for Encoding Prior Experience; A24: Geometric Fabrics: a Safe Guiding Medium for Policy Learning. The NVIDIA overview introduces F22. The derivations below are written independently with explicit conventions and qualifications.

R0. State, domain, and conventions

Let Q\subset\mathbb R^n be a configuration chart, v=\dot q, and a=\ddot q. Work on the slit tangent domain TQ\setminus\{v=0\} unless a zero-speed extension is stated. Assume sufficient differentiability for each calculation and local existence/uniqueness of the resulting ODE. Compactness, forward completeness, and regularity at zero are additional hypotheses when invoking convergence.

An acceleration generator is a=h(q,v). A trajectory includes its time parameter; an oriented path is its curve modulo increasing regular changes of parameter. A geometry of paths specifies such curves by starting position and direction. A chosen generating equation additionally selects a clock. Neither a single path nor a single timed trajectory is the whole geometry.

Use E for a positive motion-energy function, M_E=\partial^2_{vv}E for its Hessian, \xi_E=(D_q\nabla_vE)v-\nabla_qE, W for an independently chosen priority matrix, g for a conservative fabric acceleration, u for added navigation acceleration, and \psi for a potential. All gradients are columns. A natural-form pair (W,f) means Wa+f=0, hence h=-W^{-1}f. Applied force and residual force have opposite signs in this convention.

For c>0, a function is HDk in velocity if f(q,cv)=c^kf(q,v). Positive homogeneity imposes no reversal symmetry. Direction dependence is compatible with HD0. A direction-dependent HD0 function may lack a direction-independent limit at zero.

R0a. Classical robot mechanics as the starting point

Newtonian origin. For a smooth, time-independent material-point representation r_i(q) of rigid links, J_i=\partial_qr_i gives \ddot r_i=J_i a+\dot J_i v. The virtual-work identity f_i^T\delta r_i=(J_i^Tf_i)^T\delta q, applied to Newton's equations, eliminates ideal constraint reactions and yields

Q_{\rm net}=\sum_i m_iJ_i^T\ddot r_i=Ma+\xi,\qquad M=\sum_i m_iJ_i^TJ_i,\quad \xi=\sum_i m_iJ_i^T\dot J_i v.

The sum becomes a mass integral for continuous rigid bodies. Symmetry of the mixed derivatives of r_i gives (\partial_{q_j}J_i)v=\frac{d}{dt}(J_i)_{:,j}, hence \nabla_q E=\sum_i m_i\dot J_i^TJ_i v. Differentiating M therefore proves \xi=\dot Mv-\nabla_qE directly from Newtonian mechanics. With Q_{\rm net}=\tau-\nabla U-Bv, this produces the equation below; energy conservation alone was not used to infer a vector equation.

For a fixed-base ideal manipulator in joint coordinates, let E=\tfrac12v^TM(q)v, with smooth symmetric positive-definite M, potential U(q), and B=B^T\succeq0. The autonomous classical equation is

Ma+\xi+\nabla U=\tau-Bv,\qquad \xi=\dot Mv-\nabla_qE,\qquad \dot M=\sum_j(\partial_{q_j}M)v_j.

Here \nabla_qE holds v fixed. This ξ is obtained by expanding the Euler–Lagrange equation for L=E-U. Since

v^T\nabla_qE=\tfrac12v^T\dot Mv, \quad v^T\xi=\tfrac12v^T\dot Mv, \quad \dot E=v^TMa+\tfrac12v^T\dot Mv,

the total energy satisfies \dot H=v^T\tau-v^TBv for H=E+U. In the absence of potentials, forcing, and damping, the natural rule h_M=-M^{-1}\xi conserves E and is HD2. Choosing M fixes this classical rule, although conservation alone does not uniquely determine a vector field. A constant overall rescaling of M leaves the natural rule unchanged. A zero-work added force b, with v^Tb=0, modifies the rule by M^{-1}b while conserving E; it must also have the appropriate HD2 scaling to preserve the geometric subclass.

Natural versus forced virtual motion. The natural-motion specialization is \nabla U=0,\tau=0,B=0, not merely the assumption of ideal mechanical constraints. It specifies \dot q=v,\dot v=-M^{-1}\xi with initial state (q_0,v_0), and conserves E. A chosen virtual inertia M_i(q) defines E_i=\tfrac12v^TM_i v, its associated \xi_i, and a_i^{\rm natural}=-M_i^{-1}\xi_i, still without potential forces or damping.

Adding an autonomous virtual potential \psi_i(q) and B_i=B_i^T\succeq0 changes this force assumption while retaining the ideal smooth mechanical structure:

M_i a_i+\xi_i=-\nabla\psi_i-B_i v, \qquad a_i=a_i^{\rm natural}-M_i^{-1}\nabla\psi_i-M_i^{-1}B_i v.

Along the isolated component, \dot E_i=-v^T\nabla\psi_i-v^TB_i v, hence \frac{d}{dt}(E_i+\psi_i)=-v^TB_i v. Kinetic energy alone need not be conserved. For components in the same coordinates, the force-balance combination \sum_iM_i(a-a_i)=0 gives

a=\left(\sum_iM_i\right)^{-1}\sum_iM_i a_i,\qquad \frac{d}{dt}\sum_i(E_i+\psi_i)=-v^T\left(\sum_iB_i\right)v.

The last identity is for the aggregate; individual component energies need not obey their isolated balance under the shared acceleration. The metric–geometry coupling is already present with all \psi_i=0,B_i=0: changing M_i generally changes both its weight and its natural request. To study energy-preserving bending, set potential and damping terms aside again and allow only the zero-work bending term.

Freedom beyond the natural rule. For fixed (q,v) with v\ne0, the natural equation Ma+\xi=0 fixes a uniquely, whereas conservation imposes only v^T(Ma+\xi)=0. Since v^TM\ne0, the latter defines an affine hyperplane of dimension n−1:

\{a:\dot E=0\}=h_M+\ker(v^TM).

Writing Ma+\xi=b, this becomes v^Tb=0 or equivalently v^TM\Delta a=0, where \Delta a=M^{-1}b. Conservation over a trajectory requires this condition throughout, not merely at its initial state. At v = 0 the instantaneous power constraint is vacuous and does not resolve extension of the motion law through rest.

For a two-joint virtual model M = I, ξ = 0, the smooth rule b=\lambda(q,v)(-v_2,v_1)^T has v^Tb=0 identically. At v=(1,0)^T,\lambda=2, it changes a from zero to (0,2)^T. A constant applied force (0,2)^T would instead yield v(t)=(1,2t)^T, E(t)=1/2+2t^2, demonstrating why initial zero power is insufficient. A nonzero constant λ in the rotating-force rule gives HD1, not HD2; choosing λ positively HD1 makes b HD2 on its regular domain. Thus energy-preserving freedom alone does not imply a geometric fabric. Reintroducing potential and damping gives \frac{d}{dt}(E+\psi)=v^Tb-v^TBv=-v^TBv under the same zero-work condition, without by itself establishing convergence.

Explicit 2R example. Two massless links of unit length carry unit point masses at the elbow and tip. In a horizontal plane, p_1=(\cos q_1,\sin q_1) and p_2=p_1+(\cos(q_1+q_2),\sin(q_1+q_2)). From E=(\|\dot p_1\|^2+\|\dot p_2\|^2)/2,

M=\begin{bmatrix}3+2\cos q_2&1+\cos q_2\\1+\cos q_2&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad \xi=\begin{bmatrix}-\sin q_2(2v_1v_2+v_2^2)\\\sin q_2v_1^2\end{bmatrix}.

The determinant is 2-\cos^2q_2\ge1. At q=(0,\pi/2),v=(1,0), h_M=(1/2,-3/2) and E=3/2. A constant identity virtual inertia instead has \bar\xi=0 and zero natural acceleration. Their initial acceleration difference is not parallel to v, so their local joint-space paths differ. The identity model conserves its own virtual energy, not the physical point-mass arm's energy in general. These calculations are an original teaching example of the classical structure discussed in F22 §III-B; see also the MIT manipulator equations.

R1. Why HD2 describes a geometry

Proposition R1a (constant clock scaling). The generator has exact constant clock equivariance for every initial state if and only if it is HD2.

Proof. If q(t) solves \ddot q=h(q,\dot q), define q_c(t)=q(ct). Then

\dot q_c=c\dot q(ct),\qquad \ddot q_c=c^2h(q(ct),\dot q(ct)).

The same ODE requires \ddot q_c=h(q(ct),c\dot q(ct)). Equality at every possible initial state is precisely h(q,cv)=c^2h(q,v). ∎

Proposition R1b (projective freedom). For HD2 h, adding a scalar tangential term \lambda(q,v)v preserves the local oriented path family wherever the clock remains regular and increasing.

Proof. Let r''(\tau)=h(r,r'), and let q(t)=r(\tau(t)), \dot\tau>0. Then

v=r'\dot\tau,\quad a=r''\dot\tau^2+r'\ddot\tau =h(q,v)+\frac{\ddot\tau}{\dot\tau}v.

Conversely the scalar clock equation \ddot\tau/\dot\tau=\lambda(r(\tau),r'(\tau)\dot\tau), together with \dot\tau(0)>0, has a locally increasing solution under the usual ODE conditions. Thus the projected equation

P_v^\perp(a-h)=0,\qquad P_v^\perp=I-\frac{vv^T}{v^Tv},

describes the geometry without fixing tangential acceleration. ∎

For prescribed \lambda(t), the clock rate is \dot\tau(t)=\dot\tau(0)\exp(\int_0^t\lambda(u)\,du), positive on its regular finite domain when its initial value is positive. For autonomous \lambda(q,v), the full acceleration h+\lambda v remains HD2 exactly when λ is HD1 on the nonzero-velocity domain; arbitrary tangential speed control need not preserve HD2 of the full policy. The base generator itself can have tangential acceleration: the equivalence concerns its path geometry, not a claim that each summand has an exclusive turning or timing role.

The unchanged HD2 ODE admits constant clock scaling; nonuniform clock changes generally produce the additional tangential term. For example, a=-\beta v, with constant \beta>0, has q(t)=q_0+(1-e^{-\beta t})v_0/\beta. Its paths are straight over the portions traversed, but r(t)=q(ct) obeys \ddot r=-c\beta\dot r, not the same damping equation unless c = 1. Its forward displacement is bounded by v_0/\beta; equivalence of the local path geometry does not imply equal maximal forward image sets. The geometric transverse part has the speed-squared scaling. In arc-length coordinates, q=r(\ell(t)), s=\dot\ell, T=r', this is

v=sT,\qquad a=\dot sT+s^2\frac{dT}{d\ell},\qquad T^T\frac{dT}{d\ell}=0.

Stopping and restarting require a zero-speed model and a new departure direction; they are not regular reparameterizations. [G21 §II; F23 Definition II.11 and Proposition II.13.]

R2. Finsler energy and the exact energy-rate identity

Definition R2. A strongly convex Finsler length function F(q,v) is positive for v\ne0, positively HD1, smooth on the slit tangent domain, with E=\tfrac12F^2 having positive-definite velocity Hessian M_E. E is positive and HD2. These are the usable nonsingular assumptions; semidefinite leaves require an invertible aggregate before resolving accelerations.

The length \int F(q,\dot q)dt is invariant under increasing reparameterization, since F(q,r'\dot\tau)=\dot\tau F(q,r'). This differs from a timed action \int E\,dt.

Proposition R2a (homogeneous identities).

v^T\nabla_vE=2E,\qquad \nabla_vE=M_Ev,\qquad E=\tfrac12v^TM_Ev.

Moreover M_E is HD0, \xi_E is HD2, and h_E=-M_E^{-1}\xi_E is HD2.

Proof. Differentiate E(q,cv)=c^2E(q,v) in c at 1 for the first identity, then in v to obtain \nabla_vE+M_Ev=2\nabla_vE. The third follows. Each velocity derivative reduces the degree by one; position derivatives do not change degree. The stated scalings follow. ∎

Proposition R2b (energy rate). For any acceleration a,

\boxed{\dot E=v^T(M_Ea+\xi_E).} \tag{R2}

Proof. For a general autonomous Lagrangian L, set p=\nabla_vL. The product and chain rules give

\dot L=(\nabla_qL)^Tv+p^Ta,\qquad \frac{d}{dt}(v^Tp)=a^Tp+v^T\dot p.

Subtracting cancels p^Ta=a^Tp, motivating the energy function H=v^Tp-L and giving the identity

\dot H=v^T(\dot p-\nabla_qL) =v^T\left(\frac{d}{dt}\nabla_vL-\nabla_qL\right).

For L=E, R2a gives H=2E-E=E; expansion of the bracket gives M_Ea+\xi_E. This identity holds for any a, before imposing the Euler–Lagrange equation. ∎

Euler–Lagrange dynamics M_Ea+\xi_E=0 consequently conserves E. Its variational derivation follows by perturbing q_\epsilon=q+\epsilon\eta at fixed endpoints in S[q]=\int E(q,\dot q)dt, differentiating in \epsilon, integrating (\nabla_vE)^T\dot\eta by parts, and requiring zero first variation for every interior \eta. Stationarity is not global minimization.

Proposition R2c (length and energy geodesics). Euler–Lagrange trajectories of E are constant-F parameterizations of stationary-length paths. Conversely, a regular stationary-length path reparameterized to constant F solves the energy equations.

Proof. Since E=F^2/2,

\operatorname{EL}[E]=F\operatorname{EL}[F]+\dot F\nabla_vF.

An energy geodesic conserves E, hence F, so its length Euler–Lagrange expression vanishes. Conversely constant F and \operatorname{EL}[F]=0 imply \operatorname{EL}[E]=0. ∎

Riemannian energy is the special case E=\tfrac12v^TM(q)v. A nonquadratic example is F=\|v\|+b^Tv, \|b\|<1. With u=v/\|v\| and d=1+b^Tu>0,

M_E=(u+b)(u+b)^T+d(I-uu^T).

For z=tu+w, w\perp u, z^TM_Ez=(dt+b^Tw)^2+d\|w\|^2>0. Thus this is a valid direction-dependent example. An arbitrary SPD HD0 W need not be an energy Hessian; writing \tfrac12v^TWv does not in general make its Hessian equal W. [G21 §III; F22 §III-A.]

R3. Energization, uniqueness, and bending

Theorem R3 (energization). Given a generator h and a Finsler energy E, the unique correction parallel to nonzero velocity that conserves E is

g=h+\alpha_Ev,\qquad \boxed{\alpha_E=-\frac{v^T(M_Eh+\xi_E)}{v^TM_Ev}.} \tag{R3}

If h is HD2, g is HD2, conserves E, and generates the same local oriented paths as h.

Proof. Substituting a=h+\alpha v into R2 gives \dot E=N+\alpha Z, where N=v^T(M_Eh+\xi_E) and Z=v^TM_Ev>0. The unique zero is \alpha=-N/Z. For HD2 h, N is HD3 and Z HD2, so \alpha_E is HD1 and g HD2. R1b proves preservation of paths. ∎

For Euclidean energy, g=(I-vv^T/(v^Tv))h. For general energy, g is not generally Euclidean perpendicular to v, since conservation is the identity R2, including \xi_E.

Corollary R3a (completeness relative to an energy). Every regular HD2 generator can be converted to an energy-conserving representative of its geometry for any admissible E. The representative is unique within its tangential equivalence class for that E: if g+\lambda v also conserves E then \lambda Z=0, hence \lambda=0.

Corollary R3b (bent-Finsler equivalence). Define an applied bending force b=M_Eg+\xi_E. Then

M_Ea+\xi_E=b,\qquad v^Tb=0.

If h is HD2, b is HD2. Conversely any HD2 zero-work b gives an HD2 energy-conserving acceleration g=M_E^{-1}(b-\xi_E). Thus the energized-HD2 and bent-Finsler constructions coincide under the stated assumptions. The resulting paths need not be geodesics of the chosen E; bending is precisely the freedom to change them.

Under bounded angular dependence and uniform positive definiteness of M_E, HD2 h and \xi_E imply N=O(\|v\|^3), Z\ge m\|v\|^2, and \alpha_Ev=O(\|v\|^2). Hence g extends continuously by g(q,0)=0. A regular ODE extension needs appropriate angular regularity as well. Energy conservation plus positivity also forbids spontaneous departure from rest for a well-posed unforced system.

F23 uses fabric broadly for an autonomous conservative second-order system, with geometric fabric for the path-consistent subclass. F22 emphasizes the equivalent bent-Finsler construction. Energization of a non-HD2 generator may conserve energy while changing its original paths. [F22 Theorem IV.5; F23 Definition II.9.]

Corollary R3c (the clock selected by energy). Let r(σ) parameterize a regular oriented path, and let E be an admissible Finsler energy. For a positive level E₀, the increasing clock of q(t) = r(σ(t)) that conserves E satisfies

E(q,\dot q)=\dot\sigma^2 E(r(\sigma),r'(\sigma))=E_0, \qquad \boxed{\dot\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{E_0}{E(r(\sigma),r'(\sigma))}}.} \tag{R3c}

Proof. The chain rule gives \dot q=r'\dot\sigma; positive HD2 of E gives the first equality. Positivity and forward orientation select the positive root. For smooth regular r and E, the positive right-hand side gives a locally unique clock after specifying σ(0). If r belongs to h's geometry, R1b says the resulting acceleration differs from h only tangentially. Since it conserves E, uniqueness in R3 forces that acceleration to equal g. ∎

For Euclidean E and an arc-length parameter σ, this reduces to \dot\sigma=\sqrt{2E_0}. For r(\sigma)=(\sigma,\kappa\sigma^2/2)^T, E=\tfrac12(v_x^2+mv_y^2), and E_0=1/2, it gives \dot\sigma=(1+m\kappa^2\sigma^2)^{-1/2}. This does not require r to be a geodesic of E. The same E also defines the distinct natural generator -M_E^{-1}\xi_E; only choosing that generator imposes its geodesics. E is a function; E₀ is its initial value. An arbitrary alternative clock generally changes E rather than conserving it.

R4. Policy forcing, decomposition, and energy regulation

For a conservative fabric g and an added acceleration u,

a=g+u,\qquad \dot E=v^TM_Eu.

At nonzero v set Z=v^TM_Ev, p=v^TM_Eu. There is a unique decomposition

u=u_\perp+u_\parallel,\quad u_\parallel=\frac pZ v,\quad u_\perp=\left(I-\frac{vv^TM_E}{Z}\right)u, \quad v^TM_Eu_\perp=0.

The transverse term preserves the chosen energy instantaneously; the tangential term accounts for its rate. This is M_E-orthogonality, not necessarily Euclidean orthogonality. For an unforced geometric generator, a scalar damping term -\beta v preserves its path family and yields \dot E=-\beta Z. Once an independent goal force is present, changing damping can alter the full forced trajectory's path.

Proposition R4 (energy cap). Suppose g and u define a regular forward solution, E(0)\le E_{\max}, \beta\ge0, and \gamma(E)\in[0,1] satisfies \gamma(E_{\max})=1. Define

a=g+u-\gamma(E)\frac pZ v-\beta v.

Then \dot E=(1-\gamma)p-\beta Z; at the upper boundary it is nonpositive. The energy sublevel is forward invariant under the regularity needed for this boundary argument. A choice such as \gamma(E)=\min(1,E/E_{\max}) makes the correction vanish continuously at v = 0 for bounded u and uniformly positive metric, because \gamma=O(\|v\|^2). This gives a well-defined start-from-rest extension when the other terms are regular. ∎

This is energy bounding, not goal convergence or position bounding. If E is uniformly comparable to \|v\|^2, it bounds speed. A free particle can still travel arbitrarily far. Fixed-energy regulation similarly uses \dot E=r(E) by choosing a scalar tangential coefficient (r(E)-p)/Z; starts, zeros, and boundedness require separate treatment.

An execution energy K can differ from the design energy E; for the derivative formula below, K is an admissible Finsler (positive, strongly convex, HD2) energy. For example, E may shape directional preferences while K=\|v\|^2/2 measures ordinary execution speed. For a=g+u+\lambda v, let N_K=v^T[M_K(g+u)+\xi_K] and Z_K=v^TM_Kv. Then \dot K=N_K+\lambda Z_K, so \lambda=(r(K)-N_K)/Z_K realizes a desired rate where defined. This preserves the unforced geometry when the total modification is tangential, but it can inject E; coexistence with a goal-convergence proof requires an admissible damping/energy budget. Desired execution speed and a Lyapunov decrease condition cannot be assumed compatible without checking both inequalities. [F22 §V-A; F23 §II and Propositions III.1–III.3.]

R5. Task maps, pullback, and composition

For a static smooth map x_i=\phi_i(q), put J_i=D\phi_i, w_i=J_iv, and c_i=\dot J_iv=D^2\phi_i[v,v]. Then

\dot x_i=w_i,\qquad\ddot x_i=J_ia+c_i.

Assume every leaf quantity is defined and sufficiently regular at the attained task state (x_i,w_i). A nonzero root velocity can map to zero task velocity; nonquadratic Finsler leaves therefore require special domain care. Given task generators h_i(x_i,w_i) and symmetric PSD priorities W_i, minimize the instantaneous acceleration discrepancy

\mathcal C(a)=\tfrac12\sum_i(J_ia+c_i-h_i)^TW_i(J_ia+c_i-h_i).

Weights are evaluated at the current state, so they are constants in this minimization over a. Differentiating gives

W_q=\sum_iJ_i^TW_iJ_i,\qquad W_qh_q=\sum_iJ_i^TW_i(h_i-c_i). \tag{R5}

If W_q\succ0, this uniquely resolves h_q. Equivalently a natural pair (W_i,f_i) pulls back to

W_{q,i}=J_i^TW_iJ_i,\qquad f_{q,i}=J_i^T(f_i+W_ic_i),

and pairs at the root add. The transpose follows both from least squares and from virtual work: f_x^T\delta x=f_x^TJ\delta q=(J^Tf_x)^T\delta q. The curvature term follows from the acceleration chain rule; omitting it changes the dynamics.

Pullback is associative: for x=\phi(y), y=\theta(q), the composite Jacobian is J_\phi J_\theta and curvature is J_\phi c_\theta+c_\phi. Substituting these into the natural-pair formulas equals two successive pullbacks; constant weighted sums distribute over the same formulas. Thus a task tree and its flattened composite maps give identical pairs.

Proposition R5a (geometric closure). If every h_i is HD2, every W_i HD0, and the task maps are static, then h_q is HD2 wherever W_q is invertible.

Proof. The task velocity scales by c under v↦cv. W_q is HD0, h_i HD2, and each curvature c_i HD2. The right side of R5 is HD2; multiplication by the HD0 inverse preserves degree. ∎

This proves geometric closure of the generator, not automatic conservation of an independently chosen energy. Root energization supplies conservation. A constant SPD base metric paired with a specified HD2 base generator can ensure invertibility. It changes the compromise and should be included as a component rather than treated as algebraically invisible.

Proposition R5b (energy pullback and Euler–Lagrange covariance). Let

E_q(q,v)=\sum_iE_i(\phi_i(q),J_iv).

Then

M_{E_q}=\sum_iJ_i^TM_{E_i}J_i,\qquad \xi_{E_q}=\sum_iJ_i^T(\xi_{E_i}+M_{E_i}c_i). \tag{R5b}

Proof. Two velocity derivatives give the first equation since J is independent of v. For the second, vary q in the action \int E_i(\phi_i(q),J_iv)dt. The task variation is \delta x_i=J_i\delta q; after integration by parts the task Euler–Lagrange covector pulls back by J_i^T. Substitution of \ddot x_i=J_ia+c_i yields the stated coefficients. Summation is linear. ∎

For a diffeomorphism this is ordinary coordinate covariance. For a rectangular task map it is a metric-weighted reconciliation on realizable task accelerations. The aggregate pullback energy must remain strongly convex and sufficiently smooth on the domain used. A product of Finsler energies can be nonsmooth when one task velocity vanishes even if other tasks move; positivity of the aggregate matrix alone does not fix that domain issue. Quadratic/Riemannian leaves are a straightforward way to avoid it. Constant positive scaling of component energies and residual forces preserves these operations. State-dependent scaling introduces derivative terms and cannot be inserted as though it were a constant multiplier. [F22 §IV-E, §V, Appendix E.]

R6. When energization commutes with pullback—and when it does not

Theorem R6 (compatible immersion). Let x=\phi(q) be an immersion with full column-rank J, let E_x be a Finsler energy, and use its Hessian as the pullback weight. Define

M_q=J^TM_xJ,\quad h_q=M_q^{-1}J^TM_x(h_x-c),\quad E_q=E_x\circ T\phi.

Then pulling back the energized task generator equals energizing h_q with E_q.

Proof. R5b gives \xi_q=J^T(\xi_x+M_xc). With w=Jv,

v^T(M_qh_q+\xi_q)=w^T(M_xh_x+\xi_x),\quad v^TM_qv=w^TM_xw.

Thus the two energization coefficients coincide. Pulling back the task correction \alpha w gives M_q^{-1}J^TM_x(\alpha Jv)=\alpha v, proving equality. ∎

This is F22's generalized-coordinate result under compatible energy/metric assumptions. It is not a commutation theorem for arbitrary unrelated priorities and energies.

Stacking multiple task spaces gives a product-space energy and one aggregate coefficient, on the common smooth domain described in R5. Energizing that product generator and then pulling back is equivalent to root energization. Independently energizing each leaf is a different operation. If the leaf metrics are their energy Hessians, combining independently conservative leaves still conserves the sum of pulled-back energies. To see this without assuming that all leaf requests are simultaneously realizable, use the resolved root equation:

\begin{aligned} \dot E_q&=\sum_iw_i^T\{M_i(J_ia+c_i)+\xi_i\}\\ &=v^T\sum_iJ_i^TM_i(J_ia+c_i-g_i) +\sum_iw_i^T(M_ig_i+\xi_i)=0. \end{aligned}

But the resulting correction relative to the unenergized root generator is M_q^{-1}\sum_iJ_i^TM_iJ_i\alpha_i v, generally not parallel to v. Hence it can change the root path family. With unrelated priority matrices even the summed-energy conservation need not hold. These distinctions resolve the common ambiguity in “energize before or after combining.”

R7. Holonomic constraints preserve the fabric structure

Let C(q)=0 be a time-independent equality constraint with full row-rank Jacobian J. Initial feasibility requires C(q_0)=0 and Jv_0=0; acceleration feasibility requires Ja+\dot Jv=0. Let an unconstrained geometric fabric have natural form M_Ea+k=F, where k=\xi_E-b, v^Tb=0, and F is external forcing.

Add ideal reactions with convention

M_Ea+k+J^T\lambda=F.

Put A=JM_E^{-1}J^T. Since A is positive definite, the multiplier is

\lambda=A^{-1}\{JM_E^{-1}(F-k)+\dot Jv\}. \tag{R7}

Proof. Solve for a and substitute into Ja=-\dot Jv. This gives Aλ equal to the expression in braces. The reaction power is -v^TJ^T\lambda=-(Jv)^T\lambda=0, so energy accounting is unchanged. If F = 0, M_E is HD0, k HD2, and \dot Jv HD2; thus λ and the constrained acceleration are HD2. ∎

In force-projector form, with P_F=I-J^TA^{-1}JM_E^{-1},

M_Ea+P_Fk+J^TA^{-1}\dot Jv=P_FF.

This expression is dimensionally valid for an m×n constraint Jacobian. The tangent feasible manifold inherits the restricted energy; root coordinates may instead enforce the constraint through an immersion, leading to R6.

Unilateral constraints require active-set/contact assumptions, nonnegative reaction multipliers, and an impact/reset model. Ideal persistent contacts do no work along feasible velocities; conserving or dissipating impacts must not increase the Lyapunov energy. Feasibility is not established merely by adding a finite repulsive term. [F22 Lemma IV.3 and Theorem IV.4.]

R8. Goal forcing and a complete matched-energy convergence argument

Theorem R8. Let g be an E-conserving fabric with a regular extension g(q,0)=0. Consider

a=g-M_E^{-1}(\nabla\psi+Bv),\qquad V=E+\psi. \tag{R8}

Assume the full forced vector field, including -M_E^{-1}\nabla\psi, has a specified locally Lipschitz extension at rest on a compact forward-invariant sublevel of V; E and ψ are sufficiently differentiable there; the metric and damping are uniformly positive definite; and the solution exists for all future time. At rest require this extension to have zero acceleration exactly when \nabla\psi=0. Then trajectories approach the largest invariant subset of \{(q,0):\nabla\psi(q)=0\}. If that subset contains only one isolated equilibrium, trajectories converge to it. Strict local minima of ψ give locally asymptotically stable equilibria when the usual local isolation and regularity conditions hold.

Proof. The energy rate is

\dot V=v^T(M_Eg+\xi_E)-v^T\nabla\psi-v^TBv+(\nabla\psi)^Tv =-v^TBv\le-b_0\|v\|^2.

Conservation cancels the first group, and potential work cancels its own derivative. By LaSalle's invariance principle on the compact invariant set, the limit set lies in the largest invariant subset of \dot V=0. Uniform positive damping forces v = 0 there. Remaining at rest also requires a=-M_E^{-1}\nabla\psi=0, so \nabla\psi=0. For an isolated strict local minimum, V shifted by its equilibrium value is locally positive definite and a sufficiently small compact sublevel supplies the local stability and attraction argument. ∎

Compactness can follow from a proper potential and uniformly coercive energy; it does not follow from energy conservation alone. Monotone bounded V alone does not justify every limiting derivative in a proof. The invariant-set hypothesis supplies the missing regularity framework. Semidefinite damping requires a separate analysis of the larger zero-dissipation invariant set. Riemannian energy supplies a straightforward smooth metric at rest. A genuinely direction-dependent Finsler Hessian can make the forced term discontinuous at zero even when g extends continuously; R8 must not be applied without resolving that separate issue.

Stationarity is weaker than minimization: a state resting exactly at a saddle or maximum is also an equilibrium. Neither R8 nor the source energy argument proves universal convergence to the global goal. With ideal equality constraints the equilibrium condition is tangential stationarity; with regular unilateral constraints it becomes KKT stationarity with feasibility, dual feasibility, and complementarity. These statements require the corresponding contact/constraint hypotheses. For static constraints C(q)=0, s(q)\ge0, the KKT equations in this convention are \nabla\psi+J_C^T\lambda-J_s^T\mu=0, C=0, s\ge0, \mu\ge0, and \mu_js_j=0. They are stationarity conditions, not an independent contact existence or hybrid convergence theorem.

Barrier consequence. If the total ψ diverges to positive infinity at a forbidden boundary (for example a diverging nonnegative barrier plus other terms bounded below), E is nonnegative, and V(t)\le V(0)<\infty, then a continuous solution cannot reach that boundary while the stated energy inequality and existence assumptions hold. Finite caps, altered forces, and discrete stepping invalidate that exact argument unless separately analyzed. [F22 Theorem IV.4 and Appendix D-C.]

R9. Independent priorities: the additional damping condition

F23 permits a priority matrix W that is not M_E. For

a=g-W^{-1}(\nabla\psi+Bv)-\beta v,

the cancellation for E+ψ is generally absent. Instead use

V_W=\tfrac12v^TWv+\psi,\quad Z_W=v^TWv,\quad R_W=v^TWg+\tfrac12v^T\dot Wv.

Direct differentiation gives

\boxed{\dot V_W=R_W-v^TBv-\beta Z_W.} \tag{R9}

If \rho=R_W/Z_W has a verified upper bound on the relevant domain, W is uniformly positive definite, B is PSD, and \beta\ge\sup\rho+\delta for \delta>0, then \dot V_W\le-\delta Z_W. The compactness and invariant-set reasoning of R8 then applies with appropriate regularity and zero-speed limits.

To match the source's residual notation, write g=h+\alpha_Ev and

\alpha_0=-\frac{v^TWh+\tfrac12v^T\dot Wv}{Z_W},\qquad \rho=\alpha_E-\alpha_0.

The proof of F23 Theorem IV.1 assumes this residual is bounded and selects damping to dominate it. Positive definiteness or HD0 alone does not establish that condition. With velocity-dependent W, \dot W=D_qW[v]+D_vW[a] is the total derivative along the actual dynamics. For exactly HD0 W, D_vW[v]=0, so the direct contribution of scalar tangential damping -\beta v to this derivative vanishes at a fixed state. Other acceleration terms and the chosen state domain still enter the residual bound. This is a verifiable sufficient condition, not an automatic constructive formula for β.

For comparison, differentiating E+ψ directly gives

\dot{(E+\psi)} =v^T(I-M_EW^{-1})\nabla\psi-v^TM_EW^{-1}Bv-\beta v^TM_Ev.

The first term no longer cancels. The product in the second term is not generally symmetric positive semidefinite merely because its factors are positive definite. This explains why simply reusing the matched-energy proof would be incorrect. [F23 §IV, especially the bounded-residual assumption in its proof.]

R10. Compatible navigation fields: what the formal construction establishes

A potential ψ is compatible with a navigation acceleration u if, at rest, u(q,0)=0 iff \nabla\psi(q)=0, and -(\nabla\psi)^Tu(q,0)>0 elsewhere. This allows navigation fields beyond an exact metric-scaled negative gradient.

At nonzero velocity consider total-energy energization:

a=h+u-\frac{v^T[M_E(h+u)+\xi_E+\nabla\psi]}{Z}v-\beta v, \quad Z=v^TM_Ev. \tag{R10}

Substitution into the total-energy identity yields \dot{(E+\psi)}=-\beta Z. This is an exact algebraic identity on the slit tangent domain.

For the rest-limit argument define A_v=vv^T/Z, P_v=I-A_vM_E. Then

a=g-\beta v+P_vu-A_v\nabla\psi.

The range of P_v is M_E-orthogonal to v, its kernel is span(v), and the range of A_v is span(v). At a limiting resting configuration, take a subsequence with v/\|v\|\to e and M_E\to M_*\succ0, and define A_*=ee^T/(e^TM_*e), P_*=I-A_*M_*. Under conditions ensuring a\to0, g\to0, \beta v\to0, bounded/regular coefficients, and continuous navigation limits, the limiting equation separates into

P_*u(q_*,0)=0,\qquad A_*\nabla\psi(q_*)=0.

If u were nonzero, the first equation would make it parallel to the limiting direction, while the second would make its inner product with ∇ψ zero, contradicting compatibility. Hence the rest limit lies in the common zero set.

This reconstructs the key mechanism of F23 Theorem III.5. It does not remove the zero-speed issue: A_v and P_v can have direction-dependent limits, so the exact formula need not admit a single continuous vector field at rest for a general u. A complete implementation-level convergence theorem must specify a well-posed extension or regularization and recheck its Lyapunov and limit arguments. The energy identity plus compatible rest-limit argument should not be silently promoted into a guarantee for every numerical regularization. The matched-energy formulation R8 avoids this particular projected-forcing ambiguity, but still needs a regular metric/forcing extension as stated there.

R11. A fully specified teaching construction

Let q be a point in \mathbb R^2, outside a disk of radius r centered at o. Define d=\|q-o\|-r>0, n=(q-o)/\|q-o\|, and \chi(d)=\exp(-d/\ell), with \ell>0. Choose the HD2 generator

h(q,v)=\eta\chi(d)\|v\|^2n.

Energize with Euclidean E. The quotient cancels algebraically, giving the globally regular velocity expression

g=\eta\chi(d)\{\|v\|^2n-v(v^Tn)\}.

It is zero at rest without a numerical denominator. Add

\psi_g=\tfrac k2\|q-q_*\|^2,\quad \psi_b(d)=\begin{cases}\lambda(1/d-1/d_0)^2,&0<d<d_0,\\0,&d\ge d_0,\end{cases}

and a=g-\nabla(\psi_g+\psi_b)-bv, k,b,\lambda>0. The barrier force is

-\nabla\psi_b=\frac{2\lambda(1/d-1/d_0)}{d^2}n\quad(d<d_0).

On the interior, the generator is HD2, the energized term is conservative, and \dot V=-b\|v\|^2. The potential is C1 at d_0 and its gradient is locally Lipschitz away from the obstacle. If V\le V_0, its nonnegative terms give the explicit bounds

\|v\|\le\sqrt{2V_0},\quad \|q-q_*\|\le\sqrt{2V_0/k},\quad d\ge\left(d_0^{-1}+\sqrt{V_0/\lambda}\right)^{-1}.

The distance bound also holds outside the barrier region since its right-hand side is at most d_0. These bounds produce compact feasible sublevels on which the ODE is regular, supplying R8's hypotheses and forward existence. The destination can shift if the goal lies in the active barrier region, and additional stationary points can occur. Exact head-on symmetry removes the radial geometric turn because h is parallel to v; the barrier then prevents penetration by braking/repelling, possibly leaving a stationary equilibrium before the disk. These are mathematical features to show, not conceal in a demonstration.

For a robot, replace the point tasks with static maps for end-effector coordinates, link-to-obstacle distances, and joint-limit distances. Supply HD0 priorities, pull back via R5, and either use a compatible aggregate energy or choose E separately with the R9 analysis. Moving maps require explicit time derivatives; finite-dimensional configuration geometry does not by itself establish feasible contact dynamics.

R12. Numerical implementation and identities that cease to be exact

Zero-speed denominator

With N and Z from R3, replacing \alpha=-N/Z by \alpha_\epsilon=-N/(Z+\epsilon) yields the exact residual

\dot E=\frac{\epsilon N}{Z+\epsilon}.

Its sign is not fixed. A fixed ε breaks exact HD2. For a regular HD2 generator, an explicit zero branch with the continuous limit preserves the intended mathematical definition; finite thresholds still introduce an approximation.

Time discretization

Even if v^Ta=0, a forward-Euler velocity update gives

\tfrac12\|v+\Delta t a\|^2-\tfrac12\|v\|^2 =\tfrac12\Delta t^2\|a\|^2.

Continuous conservation is therefore not discrete conservation. Report energy drift and path residuals; test convergence under decreasing step size. A higher-order integrator reduces error but does not turn an identity of the ODE into an exact identity of the solver.

Solve and regularization

Numerically solve Wa=r rather than explicitly constructing W^{-1}. A base metric or diagonal shift changes the generator and its energy relation. Include the corresponding component in the derivation, or measure the violation as an approximation.

Saturation and moving maps

Acceleration clipping, speed-dependent gates, and changing goals can invalidate homogeneity or the autonomous Lyapunov proof. For x=\phi(q,t),

\ddot x=J\ddot q+D^2_{qq}\phi[v,v]+2D^2_{qt}\phi\,v+\partial^2_{tt}\phi.

A time-varying potential adds \partial_t\psi to \dot V. These terms must be included rather than borrowed from the static theory.

Verification

Check numerical homogeneity at fixed q and direction, verify the energy-rate residual, compare the same geometry under speed changes, verify pullback against differentiated kinematics, and test the chosen convergence/constraint assumptions in the actual domain. These checks catch implementation mistakes; they do not replace the stated proofs.

A24's nominal artificial dynamics can be read in this notation as a=h+\alpha_Ev-W^{-1}(\nabla\psi+Bv)-\beta v. Its use of a separate physical tracking system is an implementation layer, not a change in the geometric construction. Its arbitrary policy forcing and numerical constraint handling require their own energy/feasibility analysis.
